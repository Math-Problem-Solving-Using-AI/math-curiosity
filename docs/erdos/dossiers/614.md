## Statement

Saved page (about 2026-10-16, screen-reader text):

> Let [[f of open paren n comma k close paren]] be minimal such that there is a graph with [[n]]
> vertices and [[f of open paren n comma k close paren]] edges where every set of [[k plus 2]] vertices
> induces a subgraph with maximum degree at least [[k]] . Determine [[f of open paren n comma k close paren]] .

## Our reading, and what is already known by an elementary argument

Pass to the complement graph H of G. Within a set S of k+2 vertices, G[S] has maximum degree <= k-1
exactly when every vertex of S has at least 2 non-neighbours in S, i.e. H[S] has minimum degree >= 2.
So "every (k+2)-set induces maximum degree >= k in G" is the same as "H has no (k+2)-vertex subgraph of
minimum degree >= 2". That means H avoids a finite family F_k of graphs, and
**f(n,k) = C(n,2) - ex(n, F_k)**. This complement argument is ours. It agrees with the note cited
below.

Worked cases (our checks):
- k = 1: a 3-set has max degree >= 1 iff it contains an edge of G, so H must be triangle-free:
  f(n,1) = C(n,2) - floor(n^2/4) (Mantel).
- k = 2: G[S] on 4 vertices has max degree <= 1 iff G[S] is a matching, iff H[S] contains a 4-cycle.
  So H must be C4-free: f(n,2) = C(n,2) - ex(n, C4). The values ex(n, C4) are tabulated: OEIS A006855
  **[read, re-checked]**, "Maximum number of edges in an n-node squarefree graph, or, in a graph
  containing no 4-cycle, or no C_4", data up to n = 40 (a(40) = 127), with bounds beyond that.

So the open part is k >= 3: compute ex(n, F_k) for small n, and describe it in general.

## Why it looks low-hanging

ex(n, F) for a small finite family F and small n is a standard finite computation (SAT/ILP or
orderly generation). Values for k = 3, 4 seem unpublished. Certificates are easy for lower bounds on
ex (an F-free graph) and need an exhaustive or SAT proof for upper bounds.

## First concrete step and certificate format

1. **Independently re-derive F_k** for k = 3, 4 by brute force (all graphs on k+2 vertices with
   minimum degree >= 2, minimal under subgraphs) and compare with Tang's F3min.
2. Compute f(n,3) = C(n,2) - ex(n, F_3) for n up to about 16-20 with SAT (one clause per (k+2)-set),
   cross-checked by a direct search for small n.
3. Certificates: for each n, an edge list G with f(n,k) edges satisfying the property (checker loops
   over all (k+2)-sets), and a DRAT (UNSAT) proof that no graph with one edge fewer exists, checked by
   `drat-trim`. Sanity: reproduce f(n,1) (Mantel) and f(n,2) (A006855) for n <= 15.
