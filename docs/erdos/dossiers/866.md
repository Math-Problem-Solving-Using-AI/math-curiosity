## What is known now

- arXiv 2605.00040, W. van Doorn, "The cardinality of a set containing the pairwise sums of a fixed
  number of integers", submitted 28 Apr 2026 **[read, re-checked, abstract]**: "In order to guarantee
  pairwise sums of three or four integers instead, we show that one can replace the constant
  $1.2 \cdot 10^8$ by $1$ or $3$ respectively, which are both optimal."
- Its HTML **[read by subagent]**: "We have g3(n)=1 for all n≥3", "We have g4(n)=3 for all n≥2".
  It requires distinct b_i, "not necessarily contained in A", and says "it is not actually required
  that the b_i are positive". For the version with positive b_i it gives h_3(n) = 2 (n >= 4), which
  is probably where the notes' "g_3(N) = 2" comes from. Proofs were formalized in Lean with Aristotle.
  The paper also reports a search for counterexamples to g_5(n) <= 5 up to n = 15.
- arXiv 2609.39314, van Doorn and Erlbacher, 30 Sep 2026 **[read by subagent, abstract]**: positive
  version, constant 4 for n >= 6, "which is optimal".
- AI-contributions wiki (frozen 2026-06-30) **[read by subagent]**: "| [[866]] | Wouter van Doorn |
  Aristotle, GPT-5.3 Instant | 4 May, 2026 | 🟡 Partial result (Lean)".

## Statement issue (kind iii, report only)

The page defines g_k with "integers b_1, ..., b_k" (sign and distinctness not stated), while the
known values depend on whether the b_i must be positive (g_3 = 1 vs h_3 = 2). The notes' "g_3 = 2"
matches the positive version, and "g_4 <= 2032" is superseded. Both may already have been changed on
the live site.
